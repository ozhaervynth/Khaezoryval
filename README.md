# Khaezoryval

> The hourglass contained a sleeping eye. Its sand fell only when someone looked away.

## The Room between Two Midnights

Khaezoryval is a room reached through a passage that appears beneath certain staircases. The houses containing those staircases have nothing in common. Some stand beside rivers, others upon barren hills, and one is mentioned only in a letter written before its village existed.

The room holds an hourglass upon a stone table. Its upper chamber contains pale sand. Its lower chamber contains an eye, closed beneath a lid of transparent scales. Between them, the narrow neck admits no grain while a visitor watches.

There is a window, but no view beyond it. The glass reflects the table from a direction that has no place within the room. In that reflection, the hourglass has already been turned over.

## The Keeper of Unequal Hands

The keeper arrives carrying a cloth and a small brass dish. One hand appears human. The other remains gloved, with fingers too long to fit beneath the table without bending.

He asks visitors to remember two midnights: the last they witnessed and the next they expect. Those who answer with dates find the passage gone behind them. Those who answer with a place are offered a seat beside the window.

The keeper wipes the hourglass without touching its surface. His cloth gathers a dark powder resembling the shadow of sand. He places the powder in the dish, where it begins reflecting a creature curled around a clock with no face.

Sometimes the creature's wings move. Whenever they do, the keeper's glove becomes warm enough to fog the window from across the room.

## A Letter Addressed to the Sand

A visitor brought a sealed letter and asked the keeper to deliver it to someone who had disappeared beneath another staircase. The keeper accepted the envelope, examined its empty address, and placed it against the hourglass.

Inside the upper chamber, the sand formed the outline of a house. Beneath its smallest stair stood a figure holding a letter. The visitor recognized the figure's coat but could not remember its face.

She opened the envelope. The page described her sitting beside the window, watching the house in the sand. Its final sentence described the eye opening below her hands.

She stopped reading. The keeper took the page and folded it into the brass dish. The powder settled upon it, leaving a shape like a closed wing. By morning, the visitor had returned to her house with an envelope containing one pale grain.

## The Midnight That Lost Its Name

The records of Khaezoryval contain several accounts of visitors leaving at an hour they could not describe. Their clocks continued functioning. Their shadows continued following them. Yet every attempt to remember the moment of departure produced the image of an unfamiliar staircase.

One visitor drew the staircase upon his wall. Its lowest step cast a shadow independent of the drawing. During the night, that shadow lengthened until it reached his bed, carrying the sound of a brass dish being placed upon stone.

He washed the drawing away. The shadow remained. He moved his bed. The shadow appeared beneath the new one before he had finished arranging the room.

Eventually, he placed an empty envelope at the shadow's end. In the morning, both were gone. A warm glove lay where the envelope had been, filled with pale sand.

## The Inscription in the Narrow Neck

**Khae — zor — yval — ulven.**

These sounds appear upon the hourglass when its reflection is watched instead of its body. Their letters move slowly around the neck, occasionally slipping behind a grain suspended where no grain should fit.

A scholar tried to read them aloud. The eye remained closed, but its transparent scales reflected the scholar's face with the eyes open much wider than his own.

He asked whether the inscription was a name. The keeper offered the brass dish. Within its powder, the sleeping creature had uncurlled, revealing a narrow staircase beneath one wing.

The scholar withdrew the question. The creature returned to its former posture. A grain passed through the hourglass while everyone present insisted they had been watching it.

## Replies from the Turned Glass

> Which midnight belongs to the room?
>
> The one you remember without a place.
>
> What sleeps beneath the sand?
>
> The witness whose hour has not begun.
>
> Who turns the glass inside the window?
>
> The hand that has already left its glove behind.

These exchanges were recorded on a page found inside the brass dish. The handwriting matched none of the visitors. Its lines were arranged around a small blank rectangle resembling a window viewed from the outside.

The keeper placed the page beneath the table. The rectangle began reflecting an empty chair. Nobody admitted having moved the chair that stood beside the glass.

## The Passage beneath the Last Step

The room continues appearing beneath unrelated staircases. Its keeper receives visitors with an empty dish and a cloth that grows darker whenever the sand falls.

The eye has not opened in any completed account. Several unfinished letters claim otherwise, but their final words disappear when brought beneath a lamp.

Khaezoryval waits between two midnights that cannot occupy the same clock. In the window, an inverted hourglass keeps the shape of a hand above its upper chamber, waiting for the visitor who has already looked away.
